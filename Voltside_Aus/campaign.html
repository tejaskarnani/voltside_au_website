01

The guardrails come first, because they eliminate most of the playbook

Every other plan you'll read for this sector opens with channels. That's the wrong order here. Victoria has specifically outlawed the two tactics that built the incumbent solar sales industry, and the ACCC has an open review into lead generation itself. Design the campaign around the constraints and you get a durable business. Design it around the tactics and you get a compliance problem eighteen months in.

What we will not do, under any circumstances.
  • No cold-call telemarketing. Banned for Victorian Energy Upgrades products since 1 May 2024. Express prior consent is required before any marketing call.
  • No door-knocking. Banned for VEU since 1 August 2024 and for Solar Homes since 2021.
  • No purchased or rented contact lists. Explicitly prohibited under the VEU rules — you cannot buy someone's details from a third party and market to them.
  • No shared leads. Selling one enquiry to a panel of installers is legal but it is the single practice most responsible for this industry's reputation. One lead, one installer, named to the customer.
  • No "free solar" or unqualified rebate headlines. The ACCC has flagged that lead-generation headline claims which are contradicted by the fine print are misleading.

What that means for the ads themselves

Claim discipline

Every rebate figure carries its condition

"Up to $1,400 if eligible", never "$1,400 rebate". Savings claims are framed as estimates and the landing page shows the assumptions. Keep substantiation records for every claim you run — the ACL requires you to be able to back them.

Consent architecture

Two separate tick-boxes, both unticked

One for contacting them, one for passing details to a single named installer. Timestamped and stored. Pre-ticked boxes and bundled consent are the failure mode regulators look for first.

Speed vs pressure

Fast callback, no urgency theatre

Ring back inside the window they chose. The deadlines in this sector are genuinely real — certificate step-downs, the income threshold change — so state them factually and let them do the work. Manufactured scarcity is both unnecessary and a compliance risk.

The upside of the constraint. Because inbound consent is the only permitted route, paid search and paid social become the whole acquisition engine rather than one channel among many. That concentrates spend, which means the account structure and creative discipline below aren't optimisation niceties — they're the business.

02

Who we're buying, and where they live

Victorian rooftop solar penetration sits around 31% of dwellings, with regional Victoria ahead of Greater Melbourne. That leaves a large remaining market, but it is not evenly distributed — and after the July 2026 income threshold change, the highest-income postcodes are now less attractive for the solar rebate while remaining fully addressable for VEU-funded upgrades, which carry no income test.

Google Ads — the intent channel

Roughly 60% of budget. Solar search terms in Australia are expensive — $8 to $20 a click is normal and competitive rebate terms run higher — but the intent is unmatched. The discipline that matters most is negative keywords: this vertical bleeds money on people researching DIY installs, looking for jobs, or comparing panel brands with no purchase intent.

Do not launch Performance Max on day one. PMax optimises toward whatever conversion signal you give it. Bidding

Start on Manual CPC or Maximise Clicks with a cap for the first two to three weeks to gather data cleanly. Move to Target CPA once an ad group has thirty conversions in thirty days. Set the initial target at $70–$90 for a calculator completion, then — critically — switch the optimisation target to qualified lead or booked consultation as soon as CRM offline conversion import is live. Optimising to raw form fills teaches Google to find you people who fill in forms, which is not the same as people who install solar.

04

Meta — the demand-creation channel

Roughly 30% of budget. Nobody on Facebook is searching for a heat pump. What Meta does well here is reach the far larger group who are annoyed about their bill but haven't connected that feeling to a specific product yet — and, increasingly, reach owners of ageing gas appliances before the appliance fails. Creative carries more of the outcome than targeting does.

Instant lead forms or landing page?

Prospecting

Landing page

Send cold traffic to the calculator. Yes, CPL is higher. But the calculator does the qualifying — postcode, ownership, income band, roof — and a lead that has seen its own numbers converts at a completely different rate.

Retargeting

Instant form, conditionally

For people who already reached the estimate screen and left, an instant form is reasonable — they've self-qualified. Still requires the two explicit consent tick-boxes inside the form.

Never

Instant form on cold traffic

Produces the cheapest CPL in the account and the worst leads. Renters, apartments, other states, people who tapped by accident. It will make your dashboard look excellent and your installer relationship terrible.

Operating discipline. Ship three new creative concepts a week — creative fatigue now drives more CPL variance in this vertical than targeting does. Keep Conversions API event match quality above 7.5. Hold a 65/35 prospecting-to-retargeting split. And feed Meta the same offline conversion signal you feed Google, so both platforms are optimising toward installed jobs rather than form submissions.

Budget and 90-day phasing

Three phases, each with a gate. Do not advance to the next spend level until the previous phase's gate is met — scaling a funnel that hasn't proven its conversion rate just buys more bad leads faster.

Why the hybrid. A pure per-install fee puts all the risk on you and none on the installer — if their sales team is weak, you subsidise it. A pure per-lead fee removes your incentive to care about lead quality, which is the exact dynamic that ruined this industry's reputation. A small per-lead fee that covers roughly half your media cost, plus a completion fee on install, aligns both sides: you're paid to send good leads and paid again when they close.
The number to watch is not CPL. It's cost per installed job. A channel producing $40 leads that never close is more expensive than one producing $120 leads that convert at 30%. Until offline conversion tracking is live, you are flying on a metric that can actively mislead you — which is why the CRM integration is a week-one task, not a nice-to-have.

Stack

Analytics

GA4 + server-side tagging

Server-side container improves signal durability as browser tracking degrades. Consent Mode configured for Australian privacy expectations.

CRM

Pipeline with installer routing

Must handle: exclusive assignment to one installer, consent flags with timestamps, offline conversion export back to Google and Meta, and SLA timers on callback windows.

Call tracking

Dynamic number insertion

Attributes phone enquiries back to campaign. Record calls only with disclosed consent — Victorian law is strict on this.

Consent ledger

Immutable, timestamped

Under the Spam Act you must be able to evidence consent at the time you sent a message. Store what was ticked, when, from which IP, and the exact wording shown.

Creative ops

Three new concepts weekly

Fatigue is the dominant driver of Meta CPL drift. Maintain a backlog and retire creatives on performance, not on how attached you are to them.

Reporting rhythm

Weekly channel, monthly cohort

Weekly on CPL and lead quality flags. Monthly on installed-job attribution, because the sales cycle is long enough that weekly CPA is noise.

One dashboard rule. Any report that shows CPL without showing lead-to-install rate beside it is a report that will eventually be used to justify a bad decision. Put them in the same table, always.
